METHOD

How Liftwise makes suggestions

01

Working sets are the weekly anchor

Raw kilograms moved are not treated as a cross-exercise hypertrophy score. Each non-warm-up primary set counts as 1.0 effective set and secondary work counts as 0.5. Below your selected minimum, Liftwise recommends at most 3 additional direct sets at a time.

02

Use effort, then progress

RIR adds the effort context that set counts cannot. Liftwise adds your configured load increment only after every normal working set reaches the top of the rep range with at least 1 RIR in two recent appearances. Two recent hard appearances below the rep floor trigger a rounded load reduction or an easier bodyweight variation. Otherwise, keep load stable and earn reps.

03

Recovery can veto “add more”

Today’s sleep, energy, soreness, stress, and pain concern act as a conservative gate. A caution or low-recovery check-in holds volume steady; new or unusual pain makes “pause and assess” the first prompt. These thresholds are transparent heuristics, not a validated medical score.

04

Your equipment is a hard constraint

Recommendations and new-session exercise pickers only use the equipment in your profile. Machine and cable movements are hidden by default for a home gym, but remain in imported history so past work is not erased.

05

Recovery basics support the training

For healthy adults, 7 or more hours of regular sleep is a general baseline. When body weight is logged, Liftwise shows 1.6 g/kg/day as a practical protein starting point—not a prescription or a claim that more is always better.

06

The muscle map uses training evidence

Green needs an in-range rolling 7-day set total and a recent direct exercise improvement. Yellow means coverage, comparison data, or volume needs attention. Red needs enough work in two consecutive 7-day windows plus three hard, unchanged direct appearances over at least 21 days. It describes logged performance, not visible muscle growth.
